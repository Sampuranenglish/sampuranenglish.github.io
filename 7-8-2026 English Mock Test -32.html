Rule of the Day
When two subjects are joined by "as well as", "along with", "together with", "in addition to" or "besides", the verb agrees only with the first subject — the phrase that follows is treated as parenthetical and does not make the verb plural. This is one of the most frequently tested SSC CGL agreement rules. Ex – The manager, as well as his employees, were present at the meeting. (Incorrect) Ex – The manager, as well as his employees, was present at the meeting. (Correct)
Word of the Day
Equivocal (Adjective)
Meaning: open to more than one interpretation; ambiguous, especially when meant to mislead
Ex – The minister's equivocal statement left reporters unsure whether the policy had actually changed.
Synonyms: ambiguous, vague, indefinite, uncertain
Antonyms: clear, unambiguous, definite
