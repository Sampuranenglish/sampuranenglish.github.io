Natural partners: On India-Belgium ties

Editorial · for exam practice

In terms of size and engagement, Belgium is not one of India’s most important partners. However, the visit of the Belgian Prime Minister to Delhi is part of a larger puzzle of India’s ties with European countries. These have now gained salience with focus areas for India: in economic relations, defence relations and strategic ties. At the top of the agenda in talks between Prime Ministers Bart De Wever and Narendra Modi was bilateral trade opportunities once the India-European Union Free Trade Agreement (FTA) is signed — which is expected in December.

① The visit (of the Belgian Prime Minister) to Delhi … is — the subject is kept apart from its verb by the descriptive phrase naming who is visiting and where.

② At the top of the agenda … was — separated by the long phrase naming the two Prime Ministers whose talks the agenda belongs to.

India-Belgium trade is about $13 billion, a third of which is from over a century of collaboration in the diamond industry. Antwerp is a global hub for diamond trading, while Mumbai and Surat provide the biggest cutting and polishing services. However, once the EU FTA is signed, they hope to benefit from collaboration in other areas including renewable energy and food processing; the Prime Ministers agreed to set a target of doubling bilateral trade in the next five years.

Despite being surrounded by friendly European allies, Belgium has a robust high-tech defence industry — small arms, drone and counter-drone technology, ammunition and naval defence — and is keen to make inroads in the Indian market, with India seeking to diversify its procurement beyond global powers. The two sides agreed on an MoU for co-development of military hardware, and signed a letter of intent on defence cooperation. India will appoint a resident defence attaché to Brussels, complementing Belgium’s move.

Statements by the two leaders, however, indicated that the visit went beyond the bilateral to a more geopolitical framing of strategic ties, both bilateral as well as between India and Europe. At a business event, Mr. De Wever said that Europe is now realising the truth of India’s “warnings” on strategic autonomy from global powers and over-dependence in trade and industrial production, in particular referring to China. After the talks, Mr. Modi said that democratic values, a market economy, and people-to-people ties make India and Belgium “natural partners”, a theme he has promoted this past year, as he has stepped up visits to Europe and received a number of European leaders in Delhi, in the run-up to his Brussels visit later this year for the FTA. It is also significant that Mr. De Wever’s visit comes in the same week that New Delhi is preparing to welcome Russian President Vladimir Putin for the BRICS summit in Delhi. The engagement with Belgium demonstrates a new maturity for both European countries and India. This shows sensitivity to their respective positions on the Russia-Ukraine conflict, indicating that ties outweigh Europe’s enmity and India’s deep friendship with Moscow.

The editorial frames the visit as "part of a larger puzzle" of India's ties with Europe across trade, defence and strategic ties, while also noting the "sensitivity" both sides show toward Russia. A and D overstate the outcome; C invents a WTO dispute the text never mentions.

Question 3
Correct Answer: B
The full sentence pairs Belgium's "inroads" with India's own effort to "diversify its procurement" — both describe steady movement toward a share of a market not yet secured. A wrongly adds caution and secrecy the text never implies; C narrows it to a single deal, and D turns it into a legal claim, neither supported by the passage.
Question 4
Correct Answer: B
The clause concedes that Belgium faces no hostile neighbours, which makes its "robust high-tech defence industry" more notable, not less — the editorial is building a case for Belgium's credibility as a defence partner. A misreads the concession as a put-down; C and D invent claims about failed allies or total dependence that the text never makes.
Question 5
Correct Answer: C
The editorial states the FTA "is expected" to be signed in December — it has not yet been signed or ratified. That detail is invented and appears nowhere in the passage.

Analysis

9 Sep 2026

Core Message

  • Belgium's small size in India's diplomatic universe belies the strategic depth of Bart De Wever's visit to Delhi.
  • Trade talks centre on the pending India-EU FTA, expected in December, with a target to double bilateral trade in five years.
  • Belgium's defence industry and India's push to diversify procurement led to new MoUs on military cooperation.
  • The visit reflects a broader geopolitical alignment between India and Europe on strategic autonomy, even as both sides tread carefully around Russia.

Inference

  • The century-old diamond trade between Antwerp and Mumbai/Surat shows how legacy economic ties can anchor a broader partnership.
  • Europe's warming to India's talk of "strategic autonomy" suggests growing anxiety over reliance on China.
  • The timing of Putin's BRICS visit alongside De Wever's trip signals India's determination to balance ties with Europe and Russia simultaneously.
  • Modi's repeated use of "natural partners" this year points to a deliberate diplomatic strategy of courting European leaders ahead of the EU FTA signing.

Purpose

To describe how Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever's visit to Delhi, though modest in scale, fits into India's wider strategy of deepening trade, defence and strategic ties with Europe, and to note the careful diplomatic balancing act both sides are performing given India's ties with Russia.

Tone

The piece opens in a measured, almost understated register, conceding Belgium's modest standing before building a case for the visit's larger significance; it turns descriptive and factual while detailing trade and defence specifics, and closes in an approving, analytical tone that credits both sides with diplomatic maturity.
