Force of Law: On the Delhi Police

The Hindu Editorial · reproduced as published, for exam practice

The extent and the nature of police action, which continues in Delhi against student protesters, remain a matter of grave concern for India's democracy. The Supreme Court of India is seized of the matter, but it has done little so far to restrain the police, which appears to be acting outside of the law. To have an independent inquiry that examines the allegations being raised — as is being considered by the Court — is one thing. But an accountable police force would have swiftly acted to preserve its own honour and reputation, considering what is already available in the public domain.

Subject–Verb
① The extent and the nature of police action ... remain (a matter of grave concern) — separated by the non-restrictive relative clause "which continues in Delhi against student protesters," which describes the action before the verb arrives. Short form: "The extent and the nature of police action ... remain a matter of grave concern for India's democracy."
Subject–Verb
② To have an independent inquiry ... is (one thing) — separated by the relative clause "that examines the allegations being raised," itself interrupted by the parenthetical "as is being considered by the Court," before the verb "is" completes the thought. Short form: "To have an independent inquiry ... is one thing."

Its male officers in uniform appear in videos slapping a woman protester, and attempting to bring down a lathi on the private parts of a girl. The Delhi police is actively pursuing protesters who were apparently present at the protest site, using facial-recognition and other surveillance technologies, the legality of which remains dubious and controversial.

Yet, it has offered no response regarding the identity of the officer seen openly assaulting a young woman at a public gathering. The Delhi police would not require any software or Court intervention to identify and hold its own personnel accountable. All it needs is professionalism and a strict adherence to the principle of the rule of law.

Subject–Verb
② All ... is (professionalism and a strict adherence to the rule of law) — the subject "All" is separated from the verb "is" by the short embedded clause "it needs," a compact but genuine subject–verb gap. Short form: "All ... is professionalism and a strict adherence to the principle of the rule of law."

There is also a simultaneous law-enforcement sweep against freedom of expression, both online and offline, targeting critics of the government. The police appears eager to proceed against people merely for saying something. Abusive language has indeed been used by some content creators against government functionaries. If any of that warrants police action, the applicable law should be clearly cited, and due process should be followed transparently.

Instead, there are reports of arbitrary police action that appears intended more as intimidation than law enforcement, targeting protesters, their families and, in some cases, even social media platforms. At the same time, the police seem uninterested in acting against online mobs that are threatening and abusing the protesters. The Delhi police has maintained that criminals infiltrated the protests.

Analysis

Core Message

The editorial holds that the scale of police action against student protesters in Delhi is a matter of grave concern, and criticises the police for appearing to act outside the law while the Supreme Court has so far done little to restrain it. It draws a sharp contrast between the force's swift, technology-enabled pursuit of protesters — using facial-recognition and other surveillance tools of dubious and controversial legality — and its complete silence on identifying the uniformed officer filmed assaulting a young woman, despite video evidence already in the public domain. The passage argues that holding its own personnel accountable requires no software or court order, only professionalism and adherence to the rule of law. It closes by flagging a parallel crackdown on free expression, warning that any police action against critics must be grounded in clearly cited law and transparent due process, not mere displeasure at abusive language.

Inference

  • Noting that an "accountable police force would have swiftly acted" on its own implies the author sees the delay as a matter of institutional will, not any real investigative difficulty.
  • Pairing the force's active use of facial-recognition against protesters with its silence on the assaulting officer implies a double standard in how identification technology and effort are deployed.
  • Insisting no "software or Court intervention" is needed to identify one's own personnel implies the author views external inquiry as a fallback, not the primary route to accountability.
  • Linking the crackdown on abusive language to demands for "clearly cited" law and "transparent" process implies the author suspects some action against critics may lack proper legal grounding.

Purpose

To press the Delhi police, and by extension the Supreme Court overseeing the matter, to act decisively on visible evidence of misconduct by its own officers rather than rely solely on an external inquiry, and to caution against a simultaneous, less transparent use of police power against government critics, calling instead for clearly cited law and due process in every case.

Tone

The passage opens grave and disapproving toward the scale of police action, turns pointedly critical and almost indignant when describing the assault videos and the force's silence on the officer's identity, and closes firm and demanding in its call for transparent, law-based process — at no point softening into praise or neutrality toward the police's conduct.
