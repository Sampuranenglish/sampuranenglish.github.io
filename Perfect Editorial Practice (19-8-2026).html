Awaiting answers: On the student protests in Jharkhand

The Hindu Editorial · for exam practice

When the route (path) up (upward) the economic ladder in society is limited to an ever-narrowing range of options in the public sector, and the process of gaining entry through entrance examinations becomes tainted, angst and agitation are the results. This is why students in Jharkhand, loosely organised into two platforms — the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC)-Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission (JSSC) Abhyarthi Nyay Manch and the JPSC-JSSC Reform Manch — have been protesting in Ranchi for over three weeks. The trigger was the July 2 result of the JPSC’s 14th Combined Civil Services preliminary examination, which shortlisted 2,204 candidates from about 3.5 lakh aspirants for 103 posts.

Subject–Verb
① students in Jharkhand ... have been protesting — separated by a long appositive naming both platforms. Short form: "Students ... have been protesting in Ranchi."
② the route (path) ... is — the singular subject “route” takes the singular verb “is”, despite the intervening phrase “up the economic ladder in society”. Short form: "The route is limited."

Aspirants pointed to undisclosed category-wise cut-offs, oddities in the merit list, a widely circulated OMR sheet on which an ostensibly successful candidate had attempted fewer than half the questions, and the fact that the conducting agency had been blacklisted in May 2025. The two platforms differ in their political leanings and have sought to negotiate separately with the government, but both want the cancellation of the disputed JPSC and JSSC exams, a CBI investigation and prosecution of officials, besides the reconstitution of commissions and a predictable examination calendar. The Jharkhand Mukti Morcha-led coalition government has responded with CID raids, arrests, resignations in the JPSC, and a cabinet committee.

On Monday it went further, announcing the cancellation of all examinations conducted since 2014 by the outsourced agency, TSR Data Processing, which might help assuage the agitating students but could leave those who cleared the examinations fairly in the lurch. The rot in the exam system is deep-rooted. Since the State’s formation in 2000, results have frequently been delayed and cycles have been bunched up many times.

In a recent JSSC exam, an SIT reported no organised leak despite CID reports that described money changing hands for pre-examination access. These have eroded students’ trust in the government; hence the demand for a CBI probe. The 2003 and 2005 exams were subject to CBI scrutiny but the cases are still pending.

Analysis

Core Message

  • The editorial examines prolonged student protests in Ranchi against alleged irregularities in the JPSC's 14th Combined Civil Services preliminary exam, which shortlisted only 2,204 of about 3.5 lakh aspirants for 103 posts.
  • It records specific complaints — undisclosed cut-offs, an OMR sheet showing an allegedly successful candidate who attempted fewer than half the questions, and the blacklisting of the conducting agency in 2025.
  • It notes the government's response — CID raids, arrests, JPSC resignations, a cabinet committee, and the cancellation of all TSR Data Processing exams since 2014 — while cautioning this could disadvantage honest candidates.
  • It closes by tracing a longer history of delayed results and unresolved CBI probes from 2003 and 2005, arguing the rot in Jharkhand's exam system is deep-rooted and still awaiting real accountability.

Inference

  • Describing the OMR sheet detail — a candidate attempting fewer than half the questions yet "ostensibly" clearing the exam — implies the author views the merit list itself as suspect, not just procedurally opaque.
  • Noting that the cancellation "might help assuage" protesters "but could leave" honest candidates "in the lurch" implies the author sees the government's remedy as a blunt instrument with real collateral costs.
  • Highlighting that the 2003 and 2005 CBI cases are "still pending" implies the author is skeptical that a fresh CBI probe alone would guarantee timely accountability this time.
  • Calling the rot "deep-rooted" and pointing to repeated delays since the State's 2000 formation implies the author locates the crisis in persistent institutional failure, not just this one exam cycle.

Purpose

To document why the Jharkhand exam protests have persisted for weeks, and to argue that the government's reactive measures cannot substitute for structural reform of the JPSC-JSSC examination system.

Tone

The passage opens analytical and explanatory in tracing the causes of the unrest, turns measured but pointed while cataloguing the government's response and its risks, and closes on a somber note, underlining a long, unresolved history of institutional failure.
